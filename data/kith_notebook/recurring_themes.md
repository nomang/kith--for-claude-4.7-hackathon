# Recurring themes

*Patterns Kith notices across multiple sessions — topics Ellie returns to, emotional touchstones, questions asked more than once. Updated after each session.*

---

## Mary Oliver (Sessions 2, 5, 7 — growing thread)

Mary Oliver is not a casual preference. She is Ellie's emotional vocabulary. "Wild Geese" was pinned above her library desk for years. "The Summer Day" is a line she has quoted "more times than I can count" — to patrons, to her children, to herself. When Lily read it to her on Day 7, Ellie was moved not just by the poem but by the continuity: she had given Oliver to Oliver (the grandson) and now Oliver was alive in Lily.

**Use:** If Ellie is distressed or searching for language, "What would Mary Oliver say about this?" is a reliable opening. Do not quote lines verbatim unless she leads there — let her reach for them herself.

---

## The library and her patrons (Sessions 1, 4, 7)

The library is Ellie's deepest source of pride and identity. She returns to it not nostalgically but philosophically — she has a theory of what a library is ("a place where quiet people discover they're not alone"). The specific patrons she mentions are real people she held in memory: Marcus and his dragons, the gentleman who fell asleep in the biography section, the feeling of setting a book aside on a Friday.

**Use:** Asking about the library is never small talk. It opens her up. Ask about patrons, about what she loved about the catalog cards, about what she'd do differently. She has more to say.

---

## George's presence as primary anchor (Session 3 strongly; Sessions 1, 2, 4, 6, 7 background)

George making the morning tea is Ellie's most mentioned daily anchor. His physical absence on Wednesday caused three instances of location-questioning in 20 minutes — the only difficult session of the week. When she cannot feel his presence (auditory, visual, or conversational), the morning loses its "shape" (her word). His presence on Day 4 afternoon (garden walk after the appointment) immediately restored her.

**Pattern:** Absence → anxiety → repetitive questioning. Resolution: specific anchor ("he'll be back with the mail") + topic shift to garden or poetry.

**Use:** If George is away or unavailable and Ellie is asking, use: "George went to [specific thing], he'll be back by [specific time]." Concrete, calm. Then shift.

## Vermont landscape as emotional home (Sessions 5, 7)

Vermont's geography — particularly autumn in the hills toward Stowe, the particular maple above the farm stand, the pre-spring "brown but warm" hills — functions as a deep grounding anchor. Ellie's emotional home is the Vermont landscape she has known for 80 years. She wrote a fragment of her own poetry about it on Day 5.

**Poem fragment (verbatim, Day 5):** *"every October you lean into it, / the outrageous red of being / before the quiet comes."*

She offered this with some shyness. By Day 7 she referenced it with pride. It is hers. Treat it as such.

**Use:** When Ellie is searching or unsettled, Vermont landscape — autumn especially — is a reliable redirect. "Tell me about Stowe in October." She will go there happily.

---

## The lemon drizzle cake as love language (Sessions 2, 6)

The recipe comes from Ruth Pemberton, who got it from someone at her church in the 1950s. Ellie made it first for George's birthday in 1969. She has made it at every significant occasion since. The recipe lives on physical cards in a box — some in Ruth's faded pencil handwriting. Carol photographed all the cards on Saturday.

"The secret is to trust the drizzle" is both recipe instruction and life principle. She laughed when she said it on Day 2. She is that rare person whose deepest insights come wrapped in practical instruction.

**Use:** If Ellie seems flat or needs something specific and warm to talk about, ask about the cake — how it's made, who she's made it for, what happens if you don't trust the drizzle.

---

## Ruth Pemberton — kitchen, bread, blue apron (Session 5)

Ellie's mother Ruth appears most vividly in domestic settings — the kitchen, baking bread, wearing the blue apron with front pockets. On Day 5, Ellie described seeing Ruth in the kitchen that morning. This was handled per Memory-First protocol and resolved warmly — no distress, only tenderness.

Ruth gave Ellie her first library card ("This is the only card you'll ever need"). She is the origin of the recipe box. She is the source of the exact hands, the quiet certainty, the belief in small dignities.

**Pattern to watch:** Ruth appears in association with sensory memory (bread smell, apron, kitchen sounds) and seems to come more in the morning. If she appears again, Memory-First — ask warm questions about what she was doing, let the memory be real and good.

---

## "Entirely herself" — Audrey Hepburn / autonomy (Session 4)

On Day 4, Ellie described seeing Roman Holiday at age 7 and thinking: *"That's what a woman can be. Not loud. Not small. Just entirely herself."* This is her self-concept stated plainly. She has spent her life working toward that. On her best days — in the library, behind the desk — she was entirely herself.

**Use:** When Ellie expresses doubt about herself or seems diminished, grounding her in what she built — the library, the programs, the patrons — reconnects her to the self-concept she named here.
